Reinstating Linear Spatial Filtering in Early Vision from Fixational Motion Event Streams

Simin Mohammadzadehharzandi; Fulvio Missoni; Andrea Canessa; Silvio P. Sabatini

Event-based vision combined with spiking neural networks offers a biologically grounded framework for modeling early visual processing in continuous time. However, a key open question is under which conditions asynchronous, polarity-separated event streams can support linear and interpretable representations of spatial structure. In this work, we propose a neuromorphic model of early vision that integrates event-based sensing, fixational eye movements, and spatially organized receptive fields inspired by the retino-cortical pathway. Fixational motion transforms spatial image gradients into temporally modulated event streams, which can be linearly integrated provided that ON and OFF channels are combined into a signed representation and responses are aligned with the direction of motion. Under these conditions, the network implements a reverse-correlation mechanism in which neural activity, weighted by motion, converges to a linear functional reconstruction of the image spatial features determined by the receptive field kernel. Using a population of spiking neurons with Gabor receptive fields, we demonstrate that the resulting responses preserve the spatial frequency and phase of sinusoidal gratings and exhibit orientation and band-pass spatial-frequency tuning consistent with visual cortical simple cells. These results establish a principled link between active event-based sensing and classical linear models of early vision, showing that linear spatial representations can emerge from motion-driven event streams through appropriate polarity integration and spatiotemporal weighting.
